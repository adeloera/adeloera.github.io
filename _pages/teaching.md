Teaching has been one of the highlights of my time in academia. At Harvard I was the course head of a small seminar on natural resource economics and the head teaching fellow for a mid-sized lecture course on the economics of climate change and environmental policy. Across those courses I taught students of many levels and backgrounds and designed assessments of all kinds. I am an engaged and adaptable instructor, and I am particularly excited about creating new courses and content for the era of generative AI. Information about my teaching at Stanford will appear here as courses are scheduled.

## Courses taught at Harvard

<article class="paper" markdown="0">
<h3 class="paper-title">Economics 970: Natural Resource Economics</h3>
<p class="paper-meta">Course Head · Fall 2022, Fall 2023</p>
<p>A small seminar (8–12 students) that introduces economics concentrators to reading and writing economics research. I created the reading list, four quantitative assignments (in Stata or R), and six written assignments, including a 15–20 page capstone research paper. I guided students through canonical empirical papers in the field and through the process of writing original research.</p>

<article class="paper" markdown="0">
<h3 class="paper-title">Economics 1661 / API 135: The Economics of Climate Change and Environmental Policy</h3>
<p class="paper-meta">Head Teaching Fellow · Spring 2023, Spring 2024, Spring 2025</p>
<p>A mid-sized lecture course (50–90 students) introducing the fundamentals of environmental economics. It is a field course for economics concentrators, a degree requirement for Environmental Science and Public Policy concentrators, and an elective for master’s students at the Harvard Kennedy School. As head teaching fellow I was responsible for most course policies, logistics, and assessments; teaching fellows also gave weekly review lectures and held office hours.</p>
